Selling Smart Controllers and Drip Conversions

Homeowners buy lower water bills, a healthier yard and fewer watering-rule headaches. How to explain, bundle and price water-saving upgrades in those terms.

IrrigationSep 17, 2026·5 min read·By the Launch Lead Gen team

Smart controllers and drip conversions are easy add-on sales, and most irrigation contractors undersell them. Homeowners already have the problems these upgrades fix (high summer water bills, brown spots next to soggy ones, confusing watering-day rules), so talk about those problems instead of the equipment.

Key takeaways

  • Sell lower bills, healthier plants and fewer watering-rule worries, not features.
  • Check which local utilities offer rebates before the season.
  • Bundle upgrades into named packages so the homeowner makes one decision.
  • Pitch during audits and repair calls, when you can show the problem.
  • Have answers ready for the four common objections.

What homeowners care about

Few homeowners care about weather-based scheduling or flow sensors. They care about:

  • Their water bill, especially in summer, when irrigation can be a big part of household use.
  • How the yard looks. Brown patches, dead shrubs and mushy low spots bother them.
  • Staying out of trouble. Where restrictions apply, they don't want warnings or fines, or to reprogram the controller every time the rules change.
  • Not thinking about it. They want it to work, and they like checking it from their phone.

Tie every pitch back to one of these.

Upgrades worth selling

Smart (weather-based) controllers

A smart controller adjusts watering based on local weather, skips cycles when it rains and can usually be managed from a phone app. Many can follow local watering-day schedules.

How to explain it: "Your current controller waters the same amount whether it's 95 degrees or it rained all night. This one checks the weather and adjusts on its own. You can see and change everything from your phone."

Drip conversions

Converting shrub beds, foundation plantings and gardens from spray to drip puts water at the roots instead of on the siding and sidewalk.

How to explain it: "These spray heads are watering your house and your driveway as much as your plants. Drip puts the water right at the roots, so there's less runoff and you stop getting those water stains on the siding."

Other efficiency add-ons

  • Rain or soil moisture sensors when a controller swap isn't in the budget.
  • High-efficiency nozzles that apply water more slowly and evenly, which helps on slopes and clay.
  • Pressure-regulating heads or valves where high pressure causes misting.
  • Flow sensors and master valves that can alert the homeowner to a break or leak.

Rebate research

Many water utilities, districts and municipalities offer rebates on smart controllers, sensors, efficient nozzles or drip and turf conversions. Programs vary widely in size, eligible products, pre-approval rules and paperwork, and some close when the budget runs out.

Before the season, spend an afternoon on this:

  1. List the water utilities and districts in your service area.
  2. Check each one's website or call its conservation office about current programs.
  3. Note qualifying products, pre-approval requirements, homeowner documentation and typical payment time.
  4. Put it on a one-page cheat sheet for techs and salespeople.
  5. Re-check mid-season, because programs change.

Don't promise a rebate amount unless you've confirmed the program is active and the customer qualifies. Say "your utility currently has a rebate program for this, and I can help with the paperwork," not "you'll get $X back."

Offer to fill out the rebate paperwork, or hand the homeowner a pre-filled checklist with model numbers and install date. Taking that chore off their plate closes deals that would otherwise stall.

Upgrade packages

A list of separate line items invites the homeowner to cut things. A named package is one decision. Pricing varies by region and system size.

When to pitch

Pitch during an audit or repair call, while you walk each zone with the homeowner.

Audit walkthrough script

"Watch zone 4 for a second. See how half the spray is hitting the fence and the walkway? And over here, this corner's brown because nothing's reaching it. That's water you're paying for that isn't doing anything. I can fix the heads today. The bigger fix is converting these beds to drip and putting in a controller that adjusts for the weather. Want me to price that out both ways so you can compare?"

New install script

"I can put in a standard timer or a smart controller. The smart one costs more up front, but it adjusts for weather, skips watering when it rains, and you can control it from your phone. Most people putting in a new system go with the smart one. If your utility has a rebate running, I'll help you apply."

Explaining savings without overpromising

Savings depend on the old settings, climate, property and local water rates, so don't quote a number. Walk the homeowner through their own bills instead.

Example: "Your summer water bills are running around $180 a month, and you told me they're about half that in winter. So a good chunk of that summer increase is likely the sprinklers. If a smarter setup cut even a portion of that outdoor use, it would add up over several summers. Your utility also has a rebate program right now that could offset some of the cost. I can't promise an exact number, but you can watch your bills next summer and see."

Common objections

"It's too expensive."

"Fair enough. The controller alone gets you the most for the money, and we can do the drip conversion next season. Let me also check whether your utility's rebate applies. That can take a chunk off."

"My current timer works fine."

"It does what it's told, every day. If it rained last night, it still waters. If the city changes your watering days, you have to reprogram it. The smart controller handles that for you."

"I'm not good with technology."

"You don't have to touch it. We set it up and it runs on its own. If you want, I'll put the app on your phone and show you the one button you'd actually use, which is pause."

"Drip seems like it'll break easily."

"Drip lines can get nicked, but they're cheap and quick to fix, and we check them on every audit. Compared to spray heads getting knocked out by the mower, it's usually less hassle."

Crew checklist

  • Note controller age and type on every service ticket.
  • Flag zones with overspray, misting, runoff or dry spots.
  • Flag bed and shrub zones on spray heads as drip candidates.
  • Carry the rebate cheat sheet.
  • Send a short video of problem zones with the quote.
  • Follow up on unsold upgrade quotes within a few days, and again before next season.
