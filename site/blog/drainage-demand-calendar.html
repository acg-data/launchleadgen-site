Planning Drainage Marketing, Crews and Lead Volume by Month

Drainage work follows the weather. Use this month-by-month calendar to schedule marketing, hiring and lead volume a few weeks ahead of each busy stretch.

DrainageSep 24, 2026·5 min read·By the Launch Lead Gen team

Drainage calls follow the weather. If you start marketing and hiring when the rain hits, you're already behind, so plan each busy stretch a month or two ahead.

Key takeaways

  • Start marketing four to eight weeks before each busy period so you show up when homeowners start searching.
  • Match the message to the season: standing water and wet basements in spring, storm damage in summer, getting it done before winter in fall.
  • Use slow months for training, equipment maintenance and pre-booking fall and spring work.
  • Raise lead volume gradually before peak season and lower it before slow periods so crews stay busy.

Why plan by month

Most small drainage companies swing between too much work and too little. In busy weeks they can't return calls fast enough and lose jobs to whoever answers first. In slow weeks crews sit idle. A calendar shows you the next busy stretch in time to prepare.

Seasons vary by region. The upper Midwest has a hard freeze and a sharp thaw, the Gulf Coast gets heavy rain most of the year plus hurricane season, and the Pacific Northwest has a long, wet fall and winter. Shift the months in the table to fit your weather.

The three busy periods

Spring thaw (late February to May)

In cold climates this is usually the biggest stretch. Snowmelt and spring rain saturate the ground, and many homeowners want the drainage fixed before they spend money on landscaping.

  • Start marketing in February, before the thaw.
  • Pre-book estimates from past customers and last fall's unsold quotes.
  • Message: "Fix the water problem before you invest in your yard this season."

Summer storms (June to September)

Summer demand comes in bursts. A dry stretch is quiet, then one heavy storm floods yards across your area overnight.

  • Keep a lower base level of marketing and a plan to raise it quickly after a big storm.
  • Have a storm-response text ready for past customers and open estimates: "Big rain last night. If you saw water pooling, now is a good time to have it looked at."
  • Leave flex time to see storm callers within a few days.

Fall prep (September to November)

Homeowners who dealt with a wet spring or summer often want it fixed before the ground freezes and the job has to wait until spring.

  • Start fall messaging in August.
  • Use the deadline honestly: "We're booking our last installs before the ground freezes."
  • Collect spring pre-bookings from anyone you can't fit in before winter.

Matching lead volume to crew capacity

Buying more leads than you can handle wastes money and slows callbacks. Buying too few in peak season leaves jobs for competitors. To plan:

  1. Set your weekly install capacity. Example: one crew completes about 3 average drainage jobs per week.
  2. Work backward from your close rate. Example: if you close 1 in 4 qualified estimates, you need about 12 estimates per week to fill 3 jobs.
  3. Account for leads that don't become estimates. Example: if 3 in 4 leads book a site visit after the first call, you need about 16 leads per week.
  4. Adjust by season. Ramp up a few weeks before each busy period and taper as the backlog fills.

These numbers are examples. Use your own rates, and start tracking them if you don't. Our first-call playbook covers turning leads into site visits.

Worked example of lead cost per job

This is an example, and lead prices vary by region. If a lead costs $85, 3 in 4 leads book a visit, and you close 1 in 4 visits, you need about 5.3 leads per sold job, or roughly $453 in lead cost. On a drainage job worth several thousand dollars, that can work. In a slow month your close rate drops and the cost per job rises, which is the argument for cutting lead spend then and spending most when urgency is high.

Staffing a seasonal business

  • Keep a core crew year-round and add seasonal help for your core people to train.
  • Recruit before you need people. Start in January or February for spring and in July for fall.
  • Cross-train on irrigation. Sprinkler startups and winterizations fill gaps when drainage slows. See our post on building recurring irrigation revenue.
  • Staff the phones. In peak season the bottleneck is often returning calls and scheduling. Give someone that job.
  • Use slow weeks for maintenance work: equipment repairs, safety training, updating price sheets and following up on old estimates.
Review your pricing before each busy period. Material costs move, and peak season is when you can best hold your price. Our guide on pricing French drain jobs walks through it.

Quarterly planning checklist

  • Shift the calendar months to match your local weather
  • Set target weekly lead volume for each month based on crew capacity
  • Schedule marketing start dates four to eight weeks before each busy period
  • Write seasonal messages and storm-response texts
  • Set recruiting dates for seasonal help
  • Plan equipment maintenance for slow months
  • Build a spring pre-booking list from fall estimates you couldn't schedule

If you want more drainage calls lined up with your crew capacity, check whether your ZIP codes are open.
